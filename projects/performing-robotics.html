We document WaveForms, a February 2026 installation at Emerson College's Paramount Center that staged a Boston Dynamics Spot quadruped and a Jibo social robot as theatrical performers inside a real-time Stable Diffusion virtual production environment, alongside student-curated installations. Building on our earlier work using large language models to direct embodied virtual characters, we extend AI Theatre from virtual agents to physical robots sharing a stage with human performers.

The paper describes the control architecture that synchronizes robot behavior, generative imagery on the LED wall, and live audio from a single TouchDesigner interface, the rehearsal process through which robot behaviors were developed, and audience responses to robots as performers. We argue that theatrical virtual production offers a productive setting for studying social robots: the stage supplies the timing, gaze, and posture conventions that sociable robotics has long borrowed from theater, and virtual production supplies a responsive world for robots to act within.
