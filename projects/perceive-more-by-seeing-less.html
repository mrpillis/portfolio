We present “Perceive More By Seeing Less,” a creative research project and diminished reality experiment that explores methods for improving visual acuity using mixed reality pass-through technology. Working with a transdisciplinary team, we designed five real-time spatial frequency filters that suppress or enhance specific bands of visual information, a perceptual form of diminished reality in which selectively removing detail, rather than adding virtual content, reshapes how users perceive objects, spatial relations, and environments.

Motivated by vision rehabilitation research on children recovering from treatable blindness, the system reframes deprivation-based rehabilitation as a creative diminished-reality tool and demonstrates the paradoxical potential of diminished reality to enhance human perception. The filters, high pass, low pass, edge enhancement, color inversion, and kaleidoscope, run at 90 fps on a Varjo XR-3 with a custom five-key tangible controller, and were experienced by more than 200 attendees in a public exhibition. We discuss the theoretical foundation, implementation, and implications for sensory, creative, and vision rehabilitation research.
