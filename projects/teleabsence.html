TeleAbsence is an exploratory design project that considers approaches to “telepresence in the past”: illusory interactions with others who are gone, providing a sense of being there with the presence of a lost loved one or a lost period in one's life. Unlike conventional telepresence, which addresses physical distance, TeleAbsence addresses the emotional and temporal distance caused by loss and fading memory.

We articulate five design principles. Presence of Absence favors emptiness and the traces of a person over realistic substitutes. Illusory Communications foster one-sided encounters with what a person left behind, a letter or a remembered soundscape, without fabricating new utterances. Materiality of Memory treats personal artifacts and architectural elements as physical points of relation. Traces of Reflection invites connection with a person's inner life through what remains of it. Remote Time describes the capacity of media and environments to transport an individual back to a specific shared moment.

Together these principles distinguish TeleAbsence from conventional telepresence and from approaches that use generative media to synthesize the deceased, and they motivate a family of interfaces for connecting people to past places, people, and events.
