Semantic maps for domestic robots typically encode what an object is, rarely what it means to the people who live in the environment it navigates. We present a three-stage pipeline built on the Jefferson Drive Dataset, a high-fidelity reconstruction of a single-family home occupied by the same residents for over fifty years.

First, we reconstruct the environment, covering 18 rooms across four floors, 800+ individually 3D-scanned objects, and 40+ hours of digitized home film, in Unreal Engine. Second, 18 former occupants annotate the objects and spaces of the reconstruction in head-mounted displays while handling tangible artifacts and narrating autobiographical memories; gaze, pose, touch, and transcribed speech are bound to specific object nodes, producing a semantic layer of affective meaning, provenance, ownership, habitual use, and temporal state. Third, motion-captured, 3D-scanned avatars of those same participants are driven back into the reconstruction, generating synthetic navigation and manipulation data grounded in real human interaction.

Preliminary results indicate that memory-annotated regions predict motion paths in a way we propose is unique to memory-conditioned semantic mapping. We argue that such maps offer a significant benefit to training the paths of humanoid robots operating in human homes.
