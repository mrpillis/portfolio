We present the Jefferson Drive Dataset (JDD), a high-fidelity, longitudinal capture of a single-family home in Pittsburgh, PA, occupied by the same residents for over fifty years. The corpus covers 18 rooms across four floors reconstructed as paired meshes and Gaussian splats, more than 800 individually 3D-scanned objects, 5,886 analog photographs scanned at 600 dpi, and over 40 hours of digitized 8mm and 16mm home film registered to rooms and time periods, totaling roughly 4 TB.

Layered on the reconstruction is a synchronized multi-sensory record of embodied interaction: 18 former occupants, aged 50–95, revisited the space in mixed reality while egocentric video, 6-DOF head pose, eye gaze, spatial audio, tangible contact events, and narration were captured on a shared clock and bound to specific objects and regions. Instrumented “memory boxes” holding original wallpaper, carpet, retained scents, and heirlooms supplied olfactory and textile cues during the sessions.

The dataset is intended as a testbed for spatial representations that encode not only what is where, but what it means and to whom, supporting research in autobiographical memory, semantic mapping, and embodied AI.
