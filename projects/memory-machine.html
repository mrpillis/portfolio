We describe a working prototype of a memory machine, a pipeline for immersive memory reconstruction that leverages Gaussian splatting, world models, and AI agents to enable individuals to remember, experience, and interact with representations of their own autobiographical memories. Using the Jefferson Drive Multi-Sensory Memory Dataset, a roughly 4 TB corpus documenting a single-family home across five decades, we demonstrate a pipeline that can be adapted to create personalized authoring environments from an individual's equivalent dataset.

We outline a five-stage process: (1) photogrammetric reconstruction paired with 3D Gaussian splatting enables three-dimensional reconstructions of personal spaces; (2) world models support the dynamic reconstruction of spaces from 360-degree photography; (3) an interactive playground allows large-language-model-driven 3D agents to enact scenes from a user's textual input; (4) the environment and agents are composited into a single dynamic immersive experience; and (5) the experience is synthesized through generative image and video models trained on the user's own personal data.

The project is developed within the design principles of TeleAbsence, remaining grounded in the traces a person leaves behind and oriented toward poetic interpretations of the past. We situate the work within reminiscence therapy, lifelogging, digital twins, and spatial-memory research, and discuss the ethical questions raised by immersive personal-memory reconstruction.
